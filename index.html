Trade Compliance vs Customs Compliance: Key Differences

International trade involves several layers of regulatory requirements. Customs compliance and trade compliance are closely connected, but they are not the same thing. Understanding where they overlap and where they differ can help businesses identify the requirements that apply to cross-border transactions.

What Is Customs Compliance?

Customs compliance focuses primarily on meeting the requirements established by customs authorities for goods entering, leaving, or moving through a customs territory.

Common customs compliance activities include:

  • HS code classification
  • Customs valuation
  • Country of origin
  • Customs declarations
  • Duties and tariffs
  • Documentation
  • Recordkeeping
  • Customs procedures

The objective is to ensure that goods are accurately declared and handled according to applicable customs laws and procedures.

What Is Trade Compliance?

Trade compliance has a broader scope. It concerns the requirements that can apply to international trade transactions, including customs requirements and other trade-related regulations.

Depending on the transaction and jurisdiction, trade compliance may involve:

  • Import controls
  • Export controls
  • Licensing
  • Sanctions
  • Restricted parties
  • Product restrictions
  • Origin requirements
  • Other applicable trade regulations

This broader scope is the main reason trade compliance should not be treated as simply another name for customs compliance.

Where Customs Compliance and Trade Compliance Overlap

The two areas have significant overlap. Classification and origin are good examples.

HS code classification can determine tariff treatment for customs purposes. Product classification can also support broader trade compliance decisions when regulations depend on the nature of the goods.

Country of origin can affect customs treatment, duty rates, marking, and applicable procedures. Origin can also be relevant to preferential trade programs and other international trade requirements.

Customs Compliance vs Trade Compliance at a Glance

A practical way to distinguish the two is to look at the main question each area addresses.

Customs compliance

Are the goods being declared correctly for customs purposes?

This connects closely with classification, valuation, origin, duties, declarations, documentation, and border procedures.

Trade compliance

Are the applicable trade regulations being addressed across the international transaction?

This can include customs requirements as well as licensing, export controls, sanctions, restricted-party requirements, product restrictions, and other applicable trade regulations.

Why the Difference Matters

A business can satisfy a customs requirement while still having another applicable trade compliance obligation.

Accurate customs declarations, classification, valuation, origin, duties, and border procedures are important, but they do not automatically address every possible trade regulation.

For that reason, businesses involved in cross-border transactions should identify the requirements that apply to each transaction and jurisdiction and assign compliance responsibilities accordingly.

Key Takeaway

Customs compliance is generally a focused component of the broader trade compliance framework.

Customs compliance concentrates on customs-specific obligations associated with the movement of goods, while trade compliance covers a wider regulatory framework that can apply to international trade transactions.
